## Notes

Local numbers come from a laptop (i9-13900H under WSL2, turbo on) shared with other work; they only
compare two binaries run alternately (`hyperfine`, user CPU) and read the cgroup `memory.peak`.

**Step 1 - only what the queries ask for.** The queries are readable first, so only their ~76k
distinct terms are indexed. The profile then said: scan 0.6 s, counting sort 0.18 s, answers 1.13 s,
and inside the answers phrases took 7.2 s of CPU against 1.9 s for BOOL - re-tokenising every
candidate document of every phrase.

**Step 2 - phrases during the scan.** An Aho-Corasick automaton over term ids (root transitions in a
dense array, the rest in a hash map) runs over each document's token stream while it is scanned anyway;
each phrase's matching documents come out already sorted. Phrase queries now cost nothing at query
time: local 1.7-2.0 -> 1.05-1.1 s, board 1.63 -> 1.14 s. A later experiment that skipped the automaton
step entirely changed nothing measurable, so the matching itself is nearly free.

**Step 3.** Two commits measured in one board run: postings filed by term range during the scan so 12
threads counting-sort their own ranges (the serial sort of 21M postings took ~0.19 s), and a table-driven
tokenizer that hashes while it scans, feeding an open-addressing vocabulary keyed by that hash
(A/B user CPU 12.1-12.3 s -> 8.6-10.3 s). Board 1.14 -> 0.78 s.

**Step 4 - bitmaps for frequent terms.** About 370 terms appear in more than 1/32 of the documents;
as posting lists they were most of the 21M postings and most of the memory. A sample of ~1/64 of the
corpus, spread over the file, estimates every BOOL term's document frequency before the scan (a wrong
guess only changes the representation, never an answer); frequent terms get a bitmap over all 250k
documents, set during the scan. A BOOL query that touches a bitmap term runs entirely on bitmaps.
Board 0.78 -> 0.61 s and 300 -> 187 MB, despite 150 more lines.

**Tried and dropped.**
- Chunk-local bitmaps (one atomic OR per word per chunk instead of ~25M atomic `fetch_or`): no gain
  (1.105 +/- 0.264 s against 1.021 +/- 0.186 s). The atomics were not the cost.
- A second vocabulary table where terms of up to 8 bytes are packed into a `u64` key (exact, because
  token bytes are never zero) to skip the byte comparison: 1.21x slower (0.980 +/- 0.077 s against
  0.813 +/- 0.155 s). Reverted.

**Step 5 - measuring properly.** `perf` showed what wall-clock A/Bs on a busy laptop had hidden: the
scan was 75 % of the time, and inside it the atomic `lock or` on shared bitmaps was 8.6 % - so the
chunk-local bitmap idea above had been right and the measurement wrong. From here on every A/B
interleaves the two binaries run by run (`tools/cycles.sh`) and compares median CPU time. The two
changes in this step: chunk-local bitmaps again (task-clock 18.2 -> 16.7 s), and a 64-bit mask of each
automaton state's child terms checked before the `goto` hash map, because most tokens leave a state
through a lookup that fails (16.7 -> 11.4 s, -32 %).

**Step 6.** Vocabulary slots now carry a hash tag and the term length, so most mismatches never leave
the table, and candidates are compared 8 bytes at a time with a padded copy (no `memcmp`): -13 % CPU.

**Step 7 - memory.** 11M postings were stored as (u32, u32) pairs: 88 MB used, 126 MB allocated, plus
the grouped copy. A posting is now one `u32`, `term << shift | (doc - first doc of the chunk)`, with
chunks split further if their documents would not fit the remaining bits, and terms in more than
N/64 documents (was N/32) become bitmaps. A 256 KB filter of term hashes in front of the 2 MB
vocabulary table turns away most tokens that are not query terms inside L2 (-7.3 % CPU). Board
193 -> 140 MB.

**Tried on the laptop, not kept (all interleaved A/B, median task-clock new/old):** 16-byte vocabulary
slots carrying the first 8 bytes of the term, so short terms are confirmed without the arena: 1.026.
A per-thread direct-mapped cache of the last 4,096 lookups (hits and misses, 16 bytes of the token
inline) before the 256 KB filter: 1.068; after it: 1.054. The 4,096 most frequent terms in a separate
64 KB table in front of the 2 MB one: 1.073. 79 % of the 36.3M tokens are query terms, so these were
aimed at the slot and arena misses the profile shows; none paid.

**Renumbering terms by sampled frequency (dd9de9a, reverted).** Laptop: CPU 0.951, wall 550 vs 588 ms.
Board: T 0.4785 and 0.4759 s against 0.463, 0.470, 0.470 for the three runs before it. The laptop
and the board disagree on cache-layout changes; this one was reverted to keep S.

**Posting lists freed during the sort (8d63a45).** Lists start at 128 KB so glibc maps them (with
the mmap threshold fixed by `mallopt`, which otherwise rises after a large free) and the counting
sort drops each one once placed, so its pages go back to the system. Laptop anonymous peak 120 ->
108 MB; board 133.5 -> 115.7 MiB.
